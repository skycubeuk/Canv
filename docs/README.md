# The Canv user guide

Canv is a local writing workspace. You point it at a folder of markdown files
on your own disk and it gives you an editor, a file tree, and an AI that knows
your work. Nothing leaves your machine except the prompts you send to the AI
service you choose.

If you are a developer rather than a writer, the
[repo README](../README.md) covers building, testing, and contributing.

## What's in the guide

1. [Getting started](getting-started.md) — installing Canv, picking a folder of
   writing, choosing a profile, connecting an AI, and running your first
   action on a paragraph.
2. [Writing and editing text](writing-and-editing-text.md) — the editor,
   formatting selected text, switching to a rendered preview, jumping by
   heading, working on two files at once, exporting, and changing the app's
   theme and text size.
3. [Listening to your writing](listening-to-your-writing.md) — having a passage
   or a whole document read aloud in a natural voice, choosing the voice, and
   playing back and tidying up the recordings Canv keeps for you.
4. [Finding and organising your work](finding-and-organising-your-work.md) —
   the file tree, creating and renaming files, the quick-find that pops up over
   the editor, searching the whole workspace, and pinning a reference file so
   the AI always sees it.
5. [Connecting an AI](connecting-an-ai-provider.md) — choosing between cloud
   AI (Anthropic, OpenAI) and a local model on your own machine (Ollama),
   where your keys are kept, picking which model does the work, and watching
   the cost.
6. [Getting the AI to help with a passage](getting-the-ai-to-help.md) — running
   a one-off rewrite or review on selected text, running an action on a whole
   document, and switching profiles to change what the AI is tuned for.
7. [Working with an AI assistant](working-with-an-ai-assistant.md) — chatting
   alongside your draft, pointing the assistant at a file by name, juggling
   several conversations, approving changes it wants to make to your files, and
   watching its plan.
8. [Reviewing and applying suggestions](reviewing-and-applying-suggestions.md) —
   reading an AI rewrite as it streams in, comparing it to the original with an
   inline diff, applying or refining or dismissing it, and coming back to an
   older result later.
9. [Tracking changes and keeping things tidy](tracking-changes-and-keeping-things-tidy.md)
   — Canv's private history of your workspace, automatic and hand-made
   checkpoints, browsing what changed, viewing every past version of a single
   file, and restoring with a safety capture first.
10. [Building visual views of your project](building-visual-views-of-your-project.md)
    — asking the AI to build a small interactive view (timeline, kanban, chart)
    inside your workspace, and serving a folder of markdown as a browsable site.
11. [Adding features to Canv](adding-features-to-canv.md) — installing
    extensions that add panels, custom file viewers, commands, and more, and
    the trust step that keeps them from running until you allow it.
12. [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md) — first-launch security warnings, a
    missing key, files that change underneath you, and where to look when
    something feels stuck.

## Quick conventions

Examples in this guide use the **Fiction** profile. The other bundled profiles
work the same way — the difference is what the AI is tuned for and which
actions appear on the toolbar.

Canv ships in a dark theme by default. Several other dark and light themes are
available; see [Writing and editing text](writing-and-editing-text.md).

_This guide was generated against Canv 0.8.0._
